## Editorial boundary

The current public inventory contains six listable homes with images under the label `Gharghur`. That makes Għargħur the largest currently unserved locality after the St Paul's Bay edition was published, but the public guide must use the official Maltese spelling and keep nearby places distinct.

- **The old village core around Misraħ il-Knisja and the parish church** provides the clearest historic-centre context. A village-centre label does not prove level access, quiet rooms, parking or a short route from a particular entrance.
- **The outer residential and rural-edge streets** have a different relationship to the surrounding agricultural land and escarpment. Gradients and walking routes need an exact address and routed geometry.
- **Naxxar and Madliena** are explicitly neighbouring localities in the council's geography description. Madliena already has its own 2906 edition; neither place should be folded into a Għargħur guide.
- **Baħar iċ-Ċagħaq and Magħtab** remain separate named places. A view or road connection towards the coast does not turn them into part of the village core.

The [Għargħur Local Council geography page](https://gharghurlc.gov.mt/en/homepage/geography-and-history/) describes a locality of roughly two square kilometres between Naxxar and Madliena, with a rural setting and agricultural land around the village. This supports a compact-village/rural-edge distinction, not an invented property-neighbourhood boundary. Malta's [Office of the Address Registrar](https://address.gov.mt/locate-a-street-search/) remains the authority for the locality attached to a specific street.

Do not promise a countryside view, sea view, easy parking, silence, level walking or a fixed journey time. Those depend on the exact building, entrance, terrain, current access and transport conditions.

## Facts safe for evergreen copy

- The council's [geography and history page](https://gharghurlc.gov.mt/en/homepage/geography-and-history/) places Għargħur between Naxxar and Madliena and describes the village's rural setting. Its undated population figure must not be repeated as a current statistic.
- The [Local Council home page](https://gharghurlc.gov.mt/en/) records historic forms of the village name and evidence of earlier settlement, including a Roman oil press found near the Church of St John the Baptist. The page also separates documented history from local legend; a public edition should do the same.
- The council site identifies Għargħur as part of Malta's Eastern Region. This is administrative context and does not establish a commute, service radius or property-level boundary.
- [Primary HealthCare's health-centre directory](https://primaryhealthcare.gov.mt/en/health-centers/) currently assigns Gharghur residents to Birkirkara Health Centre and directs acute or immediate care to Mosta when Birkirkara is closed. Services, hours and the correct point of care remain current-check items.
- The council's [historical-places page](https://gharghurlc.gov.mt/en/historical-places/) currently contains no information. It must not be cited as evidence for individual monuments until the council publishes supporting material.

- [Berta Boutique Café](https://www.bertaboutiquecafe.com/) gives its address as 1 Triq Karmnu Zarb, Gharghur and describes itself as a café and bistro. It can serve as one operator-backed village-centre example without reproducing awards, reviews, prices, opening hours or quality claims.

The public guide may use the register to confirm locality and licensed category, but any detailed description should come from the operator and be rechecked immediately before release.

These are examples, not rankings or a complete local directory. Mutable opening hours, menus, delivery areas and licence status must be checked at the time of use.

## Map and mobility rules

- Use a fast click-to-load 2D OpenStreetMap overview centred on Għargħur. It may show Naxxar, Madliena and the rural edge for orientation but must not draw an invented locality or neighbourhood boundary.
- Never replace a routed walking path with a straight line. The village's terrain and edge streets make exact-address routing especially important.
- Keep verified walking geometry, bus information, current road-traffic estimates and taxi-price observations as separate sourced layers.
- Do not infer a coastal route, sea access or ferry service from elevated views or map proximity.
- A property page may state a travel time only after a route request for that property's verified coordinates and the relevant mode.
