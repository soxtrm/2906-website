## Between the two parishes

The local council uses Ħal Qormi and also records the name Ċittà Pinto. Its geographic account places the locality between the higher ground toward Ħamrun and Tas-Samra, Żebbuġ and Santa Venera. Anchor the guide on the two parish areas and identify nearby localities separately. Give street-level landmarks rather than a general claim about slope, parking, quietness or step-free access. [Qormi Local Council](https://qormilc.gov.mt/en/) · [Council geography and history](https://qormilc.gov.mt/homepage/geografija-u-storja/)

The Archdiocese records San Ġorġ as a parish by 1436 and dates the current church to 1584, with the dome and bell towers added later. San Sebastjan became a separate parish in 1936. Its parish history distinguishes the older church near the statue from the later, larger church. The two church listings are useful orientation points. Refer visitors to the parish pages for current service times and check access before suggesting an interior visit. [San Ġorġ](https://parishes.church.mt/parish/hal-qormi-san-gorg/) · [San Sebastjan](https://parishes.church.mt/parish/hal-qormi-san-sebastjan/)

## Bread, transport and errands

- San Sebastjan parish documents hosting the Bread Festival with community groups and council support. UNESCO's safeguarding record for Maltese ftira documents the Casal Fornaro organisers' work in Qormi. Use this as a lead on baking traditions. It supplies no future event date, guaranteed bakery visit or opening hours. Maypole identifies a production facility in Tal-Ħandaq. Treat it as evidence of local production and use its retail directory for shopping.
