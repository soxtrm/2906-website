## Why Rabat is next

The live public Nexus inventory contained six listable, image-bearing homes labelled `Rabat` when checked on 14 October 2026. After normalising localities that already have a published 2906 edition, Rabat is the largest current inventory group without its own edition. The count is an editorial-priority signal only; it is not a market-share, demand, price or quality claim.

## Editorial boundary

Rabat needs address-level treatment rather than a single “Mdina area” label. The edition should distinguish at least:

- the historic Rabat centre immediately below the Mdina bastions;
- Mdina itself, which remains a separate city and property context even where the two urban areas meet;
- newer residential contexts including Tal-Virtù and Għajn Qajjet;
- the much wider council territory and countryside, including Baħrija, Kunċizzjoni, Landrijiet, Tas-Salib, the Dwejra Lines, Nadur and Mtahleb, which must not be presented as if every address were in the central historic streets;
- neighbouring Mtarfa, Dingli and other localities, which remain separate even when services or one map viewport overlap.

These are editorial reading aids, not administrative polygons. The future map should remain a broad click-to-load 2D orientation until an authoritative boundary source is integrated. Exact street and official locality should be checked for every listing.

## Verified locality and heritage context

### Rabat Local Council

The council places Rabat directly below the Mdina bastions and describes it as one of Malta's oldest and most extensive localities. Its current material also distinguishes newer residential areas such as Tal-Virtù and Għajn Qajjet from the old town and identifies a broad rural territory extending well beyond the historic centre.

Safe use: explain why central Rabat, newer residential streets and countryside addresses are different property contexts. Do not merge Rabat with Mdina or describe the entire council territory as a compact walkable old town.

Sources:

- https://rabatmaltalc.gov.mt/en/
- https://rabatmaltalc.gov.mt/en/homepage/geography-and-history/

### Domvs Romana and St Paul's Catacombs

Heritage Malta places the Domvs Romana at Wesgħet il-Mużew, Rabat and explains its Roman aristocratic domestic context. It places St Paul's Catacombs at Triq Ħal-Bajjada, Rabat and describes a burial complex with Punic, Roman and Byzantine context. Both are concrete cultural anchors rather than proof that every property is beside a heritage site.

Opening hours, ticketing, accessibility and current access arrangements can change and must remain direct checks on the operator pages.

Sources:

- https://heritagemalta.mt/mt/explore/domvs-romana/
- https://heritagemalta.mt/explore/st-pauls-catacombs/

### Address verification

The Office of the Address Registrar remains the correct current source for checking the official locality and street attached to a particular property. This is especially important where marketing uses Rabat, Mdina, Mtarfa, Tal-Virtù, Għajn Qajjet or a rural place-name interchangeably.

Source: https://address.gov.mt/locate-a-street-search/

## Everyday services

Primary HealthCare's current Rabat Health Centre page places the centre at the Civic Centre, Triq San Kataldu, Rabat and lists Rabat, Mdina, Mtarfa, Dingli, Attard, Baħrija and Kunċizzjoni in its catchment. Services, referral arrangements, current works and opening times can change and should be checked directly; the edition should not turn the wider catchment into a distance or access claim for a specific home.

Source: https://primaryhealthcare.gov.mt/en/health-centers/rabat-health-center/

If used, present them only as operator-backed examples across different Rabat streets. Do not call any branch the nearest shop, claim a complete retail census or publish fixed opening hours without checking the current individual record.

## Activity and public-space treatment

- The Domvs Romana and St Paul's Catacombs may be used as Heritage Malta-backed cultural examples, with current access and hours treated as changeable.
- Mdina may appear as adjoining orientation, but it must not be presented as part of Rabat or as the address of a Rabat listing.
- Rural and hamlet contexts need exact-address verification; a countryside place-name must not be silently converted into central-Rabat walkability.
- The future map must not draw a straight line, generic walking route, boundary, ferry or assumed shortcut.
- Walking geometry, bus schedules, current car-traffic estimates and taxi-price observations remain separate sources with their own timestamps and evidence.
