## Naming and editorial boundary

Use the Maltese name **Għajnsielem** in editorial copy and `ghajnsielem.html` as the practical ASCII filename. The Office of the Address Registrar lists Għajnsielem as its own local-council locality. The Local Council describes the village as the first settlement encountered after leaving Mġarr Harbour and records a valley running through the locality toward the sea. Gozo's Cultural Heritage Directorate describes the village core around Pjazza tad-Dehra and treats Mġarr Harbour as Gozo's gateway.

A future edition must keep at least four contexts distinct:

1. the lived-in village core around Pjazza tad-Dehra, Pjazza Indipendenza and the parish churches;
2. the Mġarr Harbour / waterfront arrival context;
3. Fort Chambray, which the current official visitor source identifies as a private residential complex rather than an open public attraction;
4. the Xatt l-Aħmar and outer coastal / rural context, which must not be projected onto a home without an exact verified address.

Qala, Nadur and Xewkija remain separate localities. Mġarr Harbour may be discussed as a distinct harbour context connected with Għajnsielem, but a harbour address, ferry view or waterfront setting may never be inferred for an unspecified village property. The research record is not municipal boundary geometry and must not be converted into an invented boundary.

## Activities and heritage

The official visitor and council sources support three distinct, non-ranked anchors:

- **Old Parish Church:** Visit Gozo places it on Pjazza Indipendenza and dates the original construction to 1810–1820, with enlargement works beginning in 1867 and the present form completed in 1877. Access details must remain on the live source.
- **Our Lady of Loreto and the church cluster:** the Local Council lists the current parish church, Old Parish Church, St Anthony's Church, Lourdes Chapel and St Cecilia Chapel. A page may introduce the group without promising that every building is open.
- **Fort Chambray / harbour history:** the Cultural Heritage Directorate records the fort's eighteenth-century origin above the harbour; the current visitor source says the fort is now a private residential complex. It must not be marketed as freely accessible public heritage.

Seasonal village feasts and Bethlehem f'Għajnsielem may be covered only from dated official event pages. They are not permanent daily activities and should never be presented without a current event check.
