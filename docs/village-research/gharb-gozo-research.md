## Name, administration and editorial boundary

Use the official Maltese locality name **L-Għarb** in titles and first mention, with **Għarb** where grammar or an operator's own wording requires it. The Office of the Address Registrar places L-Għarb in the Gozo Region. The Local Council office is on Triq il-Viżitazzjoni. Keep L-Għarb distinct from San Lawrenz, L-Għasri, Iż-Żebbuġ (Gozo), Ir-Rabat (Victoria) and the separate island of Comino.

The current ARGUS record exposes only the locality centroid **36.0611, 14.2119** under the API's `locality centroid only` disclosure policy. It does not establish a street, village-core position, Ta' Pinu view, countryside edge, parking arrangement, gradient, nearest bus stop or walking route. Do not turn inventory wording or that centroid into property facts.

## Village core, sanctuary and outer countryside are different contexts

The Local Council and Visit Gozo describe an old rural village with a distinct centre, church, historic houses and working community. That supports a village-core section. It does not justify describing every street as old, quiet, traditional, accessible or close to the square.

Ta' Pinu lies on the edge of Għarb and is a separate religious destination. The sanctuary's setting is useful orientation and cultural context, but it cannot be used to imply a shrine view, direct access or short walk from a listing. The same separation applies to Ta' Għammar hill.

San Dimitri chapel, the countryside towards Wied il-Mielaħ and the coast form a wider rural and coastal context. Visit Gozo describes walking in the area, but the public inventory has no verified property endpoint. A brochure must not draw a straight line, assert a continuous pedestrian route or turn an editorial destination into an amenity of a home.

## Bus context without a frozen timetable

The Local Council points residents to Routes 311 and 312. Malta Public Transport currently shows Route 311 connecting Victoria and Dwejra through L-Għarb/Il-Wilġa and Route 312 as the Victoria–Għarb–Victoria service, continuing through village stops and San Lawrenz. These sources prove current corridors, not a guaranteed departure, frequency, connection or property-specific travel time.

A temporary autonomous-bus pilot between San Lawrenz and Dbieġi began public testing in August 2026. It should not become a durable brochure promise unless its current operating status is rechecked immediately before publication; the ordinary 311/312 pages remain the safer transport anchors.

## Activities and crafts without collapsing locality edges

The Malta Crafts Foundation describes Ta' Dbieġi Artisan Village as a 7,000-square-metre facility on the outskirts of San Lawrenz, close to Ta' Pinu in Għarb. Visit Gozo also associates Ta' Dbieġi with Għarb. This boundary language matters: the guide may present the craft village as nearby western-Gozo context, while avoiding a claim that it sits in the village centre or beside every property.

The Malta Crafts Foundation identifies lace, filigree, pottery, glass-blowing and iron work as examples. Individual studios, demonstrations and hours remain live checks. No activity should be described as guaranteed without a current operator confirmation.
