## Name, administration and editorial boundary

Use the official Maltese name **Il-Fgura** in titles and first mention, with **Fgura** where an operator's own naming or a search label requires it. Malta's Address Registrar places Il-Fgura in the Port Region.

The Local Council describes a modern town whose northern edge meets the Cottonera Lines and whose built-up area meets Żabbar to the east and Paola and Tarxien to the west. Those neighbouring localities and the fortification system must remain distinct. A continuous urban streetscape is not permission to move a landmark, service or property across a council boundary.

## A modern parish and older traces are different contexts

The Archdiocese records an older Marian niche and a small chapel built in 1798 and rebuilt in 1844. That chapel was demolished in 1956 for road widening. The Carmelites built another church in 1950; the parish was established in 1965. The present church on Triq Hompesch was blessed on 25 May 1988 and consecrated on 1 February 1990.

This sequence supports a concise activity and heritage section about the modern parish and the locality's growth. It does not establish that a current listing is beside the church, on Triq Hompesch or in an older pocket. The inventory centroid proves nothing about a view, quietness, road width, parking, pavement continuity or walking time.
