## Editorial structure and boundaries

Iklin Local Council places the locality on the hill shared with Naxxar, bordering Birkirkara and Lija to the south and San Ġwann to the east. The council's locality history supports three different contexts that the edition should keep distinct:

1. Upper Iklin, where the council records villa building on the Naxxar-facing hill during the 1960s and 1970s;
2. the later residential streets, parish and civic centre that developed more broadly from the late 1970s and during the 1980s;
3. the bypass and Tal-Balal road edge, which must not be generalised to every Iklin address.

Naxxar, Lija, Birkirkara and San Ġwann remain separate municipal and property-search contexts. A broad `Iklin` label must never imply a specific outlook, dwelling type, quietness, parking, walkability, road condition or distance to a named place. The exact listing address comes first.

Primary sources:

- Iklin Local Council, geography and history: https://iklinlc.gov.mt/en/homepage/geography-and-history/
- Iklin Local Council, about the locality: https://iklinlc.gov.mt/en/about-locality/

The council dates the first house, Dar il-Lewża on Triq in-Naxxar, to 1954; describes later Upper Iklin and wider development; says the locality name comes from the Maltese word for rosemary; records Bronze Age and Punic/Roman archaeological sites; and dates the St Michael Archangel chapel to 1615. These are locality-history facts, not evidence about a present listing, current access or current neighbourhood conditions. The council's approximate population wording is not suitable as a current population statistic without a dated official dataset.
