## Editorial boundary and naming

Bormla and Cospicua are the Maltese and English names used for the same city. The public title can show both once, then use Bormla consistently. The edition must not flatten the entire locality into one waterfront promise.

The Local Council describes five hills and the French, Dockyard and Kalkara creek setting. That supports three separate viewing contexts:

1. Dock 1 and the lower waterfront edge;
2. the lived-in streets and civic centre rising behind it;
3. the outer fortification, dockyard and neighbouring-city edges.

Birgu, L-Isla, Kalkara, Fgura, Paola and Żabbar remain separate locality and property-search contexts even when a Three Cities itinerary or one map viewport includes several of them. A Bormla label does not prove a harbour view, flat access, ferry proximity, quietness, parking, a short walk or a particular street condition.

The Bormla Local Council publishes its own office at Pjazza St Margerita. The public edition may use the council as a civic-service anchor but should leave hours and contact channels on the live council page.

Primary HealthCare places Cospicua Health Centre on Triq Ljun near Pjazza Gavino Gulia. Current Primary HealthCare pages and the March/May 2026 charters do not use identical service wording or schedules, so the edition should identify the centre and link the current service page without freezing opening hours, eligibility, appointment rules or individual clinics into evergreen copy.

Malta Public Transport's current Three Cities page identifies routes 3 and 4 for Isla, Kalkara and Bormla, while the live route system also exposes current stops and temporary diversions. The edition may name the operator and tell readers to check the exact stop, date and direction. It must not publish a permanent timetable, wait time or journey duration.

The Valletta Ferry page identifies a separate Cospicua–Valletta ferry service. Any later route feature must keep the water segment explicitly labelled as a ferry, use verified ferry geometry and leave schedules, fares and operating conditions to the live operator. The neighbourhood map itself should draw no walking line across Grand Harbour.

## Activities and heritage

The Public Works Department's December 2024 release identifies Dock Number 1 as a Bormla public space and industrial-maritime landmark and records a new lighting system. This supports a Dock 1 orientation section, not a promise about present event programming, access at every point, safety, a listing's view or night-time conditions.

The Ministry for Culture's Explore Malta directory lists Bormla heritage points including Notre Dame Gate, the Immaculate Conception Parish Church and Bir Mula Heritage.
