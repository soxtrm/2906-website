## Naming and editorial boundary

Use the Maltese locality name **Ħal Għaxaq** in editorial copy and `ghaxaq.html` as the practical ASCII filename. The Office of the Address Registrar and the Southern Region each list Għaxaq as its own local-council locality. The current Local Council describes it as a Southern Region village with a mainly residential settlement surrounded by agricultural land. This supports a village-and-rural-edge description, not a promise that every address is quiet, open-facing or beside fields.

A future edition should keep at least four contexts distinct:

1. the lived-in civic core and Santa Marija / parish-church setting;
2. Labour Avenue as a public-service and everyday-life spine;
3. the Bir id-Deheb / Dawret Ħal Għaxaq road context represented in current licensed-establishment addresses;
4. the outer agricultural lanes, which must not be projected onto a home without an exact verified address.

The University of Malta local-history record identifies Żejtun, Gudja, Santa Luċija, Marsaxlokk and Birżebbuġa as neighbouring localities. Those places remain separate editions and may not be absorbed into Ħal Għaxaq. The research record is not municipal boundary geometry; a future map must not trace an invented boundary from prose.

A 2019 government release is useful historic context, but its future-tense construction wording must not be reused as a current works notice.
