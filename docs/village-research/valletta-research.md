## Editorial boundary and place character

Use **Il-Belt Valletta** at first Maltese mention and **Valletta** thereafter. Valletta is Malta's legal and administrative capital and a living residential, commercial and cultural city. The Valletta Local Council describes its Grand Harbour setting and role as a focus of cultural and commercial activity. UNESCO records the fortified city on a hilly peninsula between Marsamxett and Grand Harbour, founded in 1566 and built around a planned grid inside bastioned walls.

The edition must keep the walled city distinct from Floriana, the Valletta Waterfront/cruise-terminal area, the Three Cities across Grand Harbour, and Sliema across Marsamxett. City Gate, the bus terminus and the ferry landings are different transport endpoints. A route using the Sliema–Valletta ferry must remain explicitly ferry-assisted rather than being presented as a continuous walk.

The inventory exposes only the locality-level coordinate **14.5146, 35.8989** and `locationDisclosure: locality`. It does not establish a street, building, harbour side, heritage view, walking distance or route for the listed home.

## Heritage and cultural anchors

Heritage is part of daily orientation in Valletta, but the page should not become a generic attraction list. Heritage Malta currently identifies MUŻA in the Auberge d'Italie on Merchants Street and Fort St Elmo with the National War Museum at the tip of the peninsula. The Valletta Cultural Agency describes the Valletta Design Cluster in the renovated Old Abattoir as a community space for cultural and creative practice, with workspaces and a public roof garden.

These are distinct verified anchors. Opening hours, ticket prices, programme dates and access conditions can change and must remain on the operators' live pages rather than in evergreen copy.

## Everyday services, food and transport

Use current operator pages and avoid frozen hours or timetables:

- SPAR Malta currently publishes a Valletta store on Republic Street. Its operator page is a valid grocery example, but the advertised 24/7 hours must be checked live rather than copied into evergreen text.
- Is-Suq tal-Belt's own site places its food market, food court and restaurants on Triq il-Merkanti. It is useful as both an everyday food-shopping and eating anchor; it is not a ranking or a claim that every listing is nearby.
- Malta Public Transport's live route directory exposes Valletta termini and route endpoints. The current service-update page shows that detours can affect several routes, so no frequency, stop assignment or travel duration belongs in evergreen prose.
- Valletta Ferry Services currently publishes separate Sliema–Valletta and Valletta–Three Cities services. The page must link the live schedule and describe any ferry as a separate leg. Seasonal times, prices and last departures must not be frozen into the edition.
